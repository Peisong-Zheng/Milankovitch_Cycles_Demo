Milankovitch Cycles Eccentricity · Obliquity · Precession

About this visualization

Milankovitch cycles are the slow changes in Earth's orbit and axial tilt that pace the ice ages by varying how much sunlight reaches high northern latitudes in summer. This page animates all three cycles over the past one million years (−1000 to +60 kyr):

  • Eccentricity (~100 kyr) — the shape of Earth's orbit around the Sun;
  • Obliquity (~41 kyr) — the tilt of Earth's axis, which controls the strength of the seasons;
  • Climatic precession (~21 kyr) — the wobble that shifts where on the orbit the seasons fall.

The left panel shows the orbit viewed from above the north ecliptic pole. The right panel switches between a Precession view (the whole planet wobbling under fixed sunlight) and an NH summer view (the June solstice Sun–Earth distance, with a scrolling 65°N June-insolation strip — the classic ice-age trigger).

Data & credits
