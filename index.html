Milankovitch Cycles Eccentricity · Obliquity · Precession

About this visualization

Milankovitch cycles are quasi-periodic variations in Earth's orbital configuration that modulate the seasonal and latitudinal distribution of insolation, and are widely regarded as the pacemaker of Quaternary glaciation. This page animates the three orbital parameters from −1000 to +60 kyr:

  • Eccentricity (e, ~100 kyr) — shape of the orbit;
  • Obliquity (ε, ~41 kyr) — axial tilt; controls seasonal contrast;
  • Climatic precession (e·sinϖ, ~21 kyr) — timing of the seasons relative to perihelion.

Left panel: the orbit viewed from above the north ecliptic pole. Right panel: a Precession view (axial precession under fixed illumination) and an NH summer view (June-solstice Sun–Earth distance and daily insolation at 65°N — the quantity central to the Milankovitch hypothesis).

Data & credits

Orbital data: Laskar et al. (2004), doi:10.1051/0004-6361:20041335 (La2004 orbital solution) — orbital elements and the 65°N June-solstice insolation series computed with the insolation Python package.

Note: eccentricity exaggerated ×3 in the orbit view; NH-summer Sun–Earth distance exaggerated in the summer view; Earth's orbital phase is schematic.
